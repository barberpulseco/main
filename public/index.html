The NBA's Go-To Barber: Tarin Dhamrait

From business cards at red lights to Tyrese Haliburton's chair, how a Mississauga barber took his clippers global.

By Ethan Kellman, The Barber Pulse

Somewhere above downtown Toronto, an NBA All-Star is waiting on one man. Not a coach. Not a trainer. His barber.

In a hotel suite overlooking the city, Tarin Dhamrait opens his travel case and lays out his tools. In a few minutes the player will sit down in his chair. Tomorrow Dhamrait may be on a flight to another city, and another player.

The Mississauga native has become one of the most trusted barbers in professional basketball. Players fly him to wherever they are because, as they see it, how they look matters almost as much as how they play. He did not get there through ads or a famous shop. He got there one cut at a time.

The beginning

Dhamrait did not grow up planning to be a barber. He was unsure what he wanted to do for a living until a friend, who had been cutting hair since they were young, asked him a simple question: “Why not try it?”

He took the advice and worked his way through a string of shops across the GTA. Clients did not come easily at first. On a podcast, Dhamrait has said he used to hand out his business cards at red lights on his drive home, just to find people to cut.

His original break came from home. Dhamrait grew up with Oshae Brissett, member of the Indiana Pacers, and Nickeil Alexander-Walker of the Atlanta Hawks. He had started cutting their hair, and immediately players around the league noticed.

Dhamrait describes his growth as word of mouth and nothing more. In his telling, it is a simple chain: do good work, and the next player asks for your number.

Today Dhamrait works where the season takes his clients: hotel suites, team trips and road games. When the Indiana Pacers came to Toronto to play the Raptors, several players booked time in his chair.

Tyrese Haliburton is one of his best-known clients. The Pacers star told CBC that he has been praised for having some of the best lineups in the NBA, and he gives Dhamrait the credit. When the team hits a city where Dhamrait is, Haliburton says he becomes the planner, setting the schedule and passing along the barber's number.

His client list reaches well past Indiana. CBC reported that after the Toronto session he was heading to Los Angeles to cut Anthony Edwards. He has also worked with Canada's men's basketball team at the Olympics.

Beyond the cut

In 2024, Haliburton showed GQ Sports the ten things he can't live without, and one of them was his barber. BaBylissPRO, the clipper brand Dhamrait works with, reposted the clip and called Haliburton “the first person ever to bring a barber as one of their 10 essentials.”

His work also reaches far past the players he cuts. Their haircuts are on TV every game night and all over social media. Carlos Bonifaz, who owns the Brazykuts shop in midtown Toronto, told CBC that clients regularly ask for Dhamrait's styles.

His own branding shows where he is heading. His Instagram once described him as a private travel barber for the NBA. It now calls him an image consultant and private barber to NBA athletes, lists Atlanta as his base, and credits features in GQ, Netflix and CBC. The change suggests he sees his job as managing a player's whole look, not only the haircut.

From the GTA to the world

For all the travel and the famous names, Dhamrait still sounds surprised by how far the work has carried him.

“It's crazy to think that a pair of clippers can bring me so far.”

Tarin Dhamrait, to CBC News

His path is a blueprint for barbers across the GTA. It started with handing out cards at red lights, cutting friends' hair and earning trust one client at a time. He is proof that you can go global from home.
